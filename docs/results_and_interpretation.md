# Results and Interpretation

The results below are preserved from the original analysis run. The participant-level input dataset is not distributed with this repository. Running the included experiment produces new data; its analysis will yield new results rather than necessarily reproducing the saved values.

## Behavioral performance across blocks

| Block | Mean reward rate | SD reward rate | Mean optimal-choice rate | SD optimal-choice rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 (15 trials) | 0.548333 | 0.118382 | 0.286667 | 0.170269 |
| 2 (30 trials) | 0.608333 | 0.108539 | 0.403333 | 0.247011 |
| 3 (60 trials) | 0.597083 | 0.077523 | 0.397083 | 0.196330 |

The Friedman test did not detect a statistically significant block effect for reward rate (χ²(2) = 2.844, p = 0.241). The block effect for optimal-choice rate was statistically significant (χ²(2) = 6.680, p = 0.035).

Pairwise Wilcoxon comparisons for optimal-choice rate, with Bonferroni adjustment:

| Comparison | Statistic | Raw p-value | Adjusted p-value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Block 1 vs Block 2 | 196.5 | 0.031951 | 0.095853 |
| Block 1 vs Block 3 | 165.5 | 0.001728 | 0.005185 |
| Block 2 vs Block 3 | 369.5 | 0.988425 | 1.000000 |

The adjusted p-value is bounded at 1.0.

## Reaction-time analysis

| Block | Mean reaction time (s) | SD |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 2.879677 | 1.224175 |
| 2 | 1.574562 | 0.736396 |
| 3 | 1.100917 | 0.559675 |

The Friedman test indicated a statistically significant difference across blocks (χ²(2) = 68.450, p < 0.001).

Mean reaction time was 1.213111 seconds for optimal choices and 1.662518 seconds for non-optimal choices. A trial-level Mann–Whitney U test detected a difference (U = 1,702,386.5, p < 0.001). Because trials are nested within participants, this test does not account for within-participant dependence and should be interpreted cautiously.

## Confidence and metacognitive measure

Mean confidence was 3.508390 for optimal choices and 3.115014 for non-optimal choices. A participant-level Wilcoxon signed-rank test indicated a difference (W = 145, p = 0.000368).

The operational confidence-discrimination score, calculated as mean confidence on optimal choices minus mean confidence on non-optimal choices, had a mean of 0.390968 and a standard deviation of 0.608629. This descriptive score is not equivalent to meta-d′ or a formal metacognitive-efficiency measure.

## Learning trajectory

Mean optimal-choice rate was 0.286667 during early trials and 0.430000 during late trials. The paired Wilcoxon test indicated a difference (W = 170, p = 0.003584).

## Q-learning model comparison

| Model | Mean NLL | Mean log-likelihood | AIC | BIC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Softmax | **114.172292** | -114.172292 | **232.344584** | **237.652505** |
| Epsilon-Greedy | 125.131479 | -125.131479 | 254.262957 | 259.570878 |
| UCB | 116.615797 | -116.615797 | 237.231593 | 242.539514 |

Softmax Q-learning achieved the lowest mean NLL, AIC, and BIC among the three implemented models. These are mean participant-level criteria based on two fitted parameters and 105 trials per participant, not criteria from one pooled likelihood over all trials.

The Epsilon-Greedy learning-rate parameter remained at its initial value of 0.5 for all participants, indicating that it was not effectively identified by the current implementation.

## Interpretation limitations

- Card positions are remapped between blocks. The descriptive optimal-choice calculation uses the trial-specific `card probs` mapping, but the Q-learning likelihood functions use screen position as a stable action index and carry Q-values across block boundaries. This simplifying assumption limits cognitive interpretation of the fitted parameters.
- The probabilistic UCB model applies a Softmax transformation to UCB scores using a fixed inverse temperature of 5.
- The Epsilon-Greedy policy uses deterministic first-index tie-breaking through `numpy.argmax`.
- The trial-level Mann–Whitney comparison does not account for the nesting of trials within participants.
- The confidence difference is an operational discrimination score, not a formal metacognitive-efficiency estimate.

## Conclusion

The saved analysis indicates a statistically significant change in optimal-choice rate and reaction time across blocks, while reward rate did not show a statistically significant block effect. Confidence was higher for choices classified as optimal. Among the three implemented Q-learning variants, Softmax achieved the best information-criterion values.

These findings describe the original analyzed dataset. New participant data generated by the included experiment should be analyzed independently, and its results should not be assumed to reproduce the saved numerical values.
